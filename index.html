Board Decision Intelligence · Cyber Velocity Gap

The adversary moves in minutes. How quickly can accountability move?

Cybersecurity controls may detect the event. The outcome still depends on how quickly the organization can establish verified facts, identify the authorized decision-maker, approve action, inform the Board, and preserve accountability.

Metric definition
Average eCrime "breakout time" — the interval between an adversary's initial access and lateral movement onto another system.
Scope
Derived from CrowdStrike incident response engagements and threat intelligence tracking more than 280 named adversaries. A global average across observed eCrime intrusions, not a per-organization measurement.
Additional context
Reported as a 65% increase in speed from 2024. The fastest observed breakout was 27 seconds; in one intrusion, data exfiltration began within four minutes of initial access. These are extreme observations, not averages.
Confidence
High for the reported figure as published. Vendor telemetry reflects the customer and engagement population observed by one provider.
Limitation
Measures adversary movement only. It does not measure detection, response, containment, or any organization's decision performance.

Illustration
Using the two published averages as an illustration, the time represented by the global breach lifecycle is approximately 12,000 times longer than the average adversary breakout interval. These metrics measure different stages and should not be interpreted as a direct response-performance ratio.
Deterministic calculation
(241 days × 24 × 60 minutes) ÷ 29 minutes = 347,040 ÷ 29 ≈ 11,967.
What this is not
Not a matched comparison of the same activity. Not a prediction of any specific organization's performance. Not a measure of accountability. Not a performance ratio.
Why it is shown
To convey the scale of the external timing asymmetry that governs the decision window — nothing further.

Stage 5 · Private Board Decision Review

Test the decision system before the event tests it.

A private Board Decision Review examines how verified information, authority, containment, Board communication, and accountability move through a consequential scenario. The objective is to identify where the organization's decision system slows, fragments, or becomes person-dependent.
